# Human findings (manual analysis)

Analysis set: 1,255 of 1,299 responses after cleaning. Methods: data cleaning with a logged, reproducible script; cross-tabulation; Net Promoter Score with 95% bootstrap intervals; Spearman correlations; keyword coding of open answers (Spanish answers translated first); robustness check on three versions of the data.

## Key findings

1. **Participants learn 988 well; recognizing a crisis lags behind.** Mean gain was +1.16 for "describe 988" and +1.05 for "know when to use 988", but only +0.50 for "identify mental health challenges". 94% agree they know when to use 988 after training.
2. **Who is in the room matters more than which county.** Students' NPS is about −2; every adult group is about +75 to +81. Anne Arundel's low NPS is mostly because 95% of its respondents were students. A smaller student-only gap remains (Anne Arundel −10 vs Prince George's +37) but most Prince George's students came from one session.
3. **An engaging presenter goes with more willingness to call 988.** Presenter engagement has the strongest link to likelihood to call (Spearman 0.41); knowledge before training barely matters (0.03). Association only.
4. **Many participants use the feedback box to ask what the training left out.** 218 people asked a question instead of suggesting an improvement; the top themes are how a call works and what happens next, scale and statistics, and who answers.
5. **The survey misses the outcome that matters most.** The call-988 question was not on 77% of forms. Spanish speakers (74, most given the English form) were 4.5× as likely to give self-contradictory answers, suggesting a misread scale; once those are set aside their knowledge gain matches everyone else's.

Additional: among people who say they know when to use 988, 28% of students but 3% of adults rate their chance of calling at 6/10 or lower. Students arrive knowing more each quarter (before-score 3.45 → 3.67) and their measured gain is falling (0.88 → 0.72).

## Gaps and proposed targets

See the table `_human_gaps` (current value from this data, proposed target):
knowing is not intending; students are not engaged; recognizing a crisis lags; the survey misses behavior; questions go unanswered; Spanish speakers get an English form; quality varies by session.

## Recommendations (summary)

Rebuild the student version around interaction; walk through what happens on a call and address police/parent fears; add crisis-recognition scenarios; standardize the survey with a required call-988 question, session/presenter codes and a 30-day follow-up; add a "what happens when you call" FAQ; offer the form in Spanish with labelled scale points; debrief top-rated sessions and flag sessions with NPS below 0.
